The problem

A locker program runs on trust. Trust runs on records.

Members leave bottles worth real money in your care. The program only works while everyone’s records agree — and a spreadsheet, a binder and someone’s memory rarely do.

Conflicting records

Two lists, one bottle, and a member who remembers it differently. Every disagreement is a conversation you would rather not have.

No chain of custody

Who stocked it, who pulled it, when, and for whom. Without an append-only log, nobody can answer with certainty — and certainty is the product.

The search during service

A guest asks for their bottle at 7:45 on a Saturday. The server goes looking. The table waits, and the wrong bottle is one guess away.

How it works

Label. Stock. Serve.

I

Label

Print a batch of QR labels on standard 30-up Avery sheets. Every code is registered before it is ever used, so a label cannot be forged, duplicated, or orphaned by a misprint — and a tag comes back into circulation when its bottle leaves.

II

Stock

Adding a bottle to a locker is a scan and a tap: the wine, its label, and where it physically sits. The member’s collection updates that moment, and the activity log records who did it and when.

III

Serve

At the table, the member shows a code from their collection. The server scans it, is walked to the exact bottle and its storage spot, confirms it with a second scan, and logs the pull. The member gets a note — and a chance to rate the bottle.

The ledger

Every member, every locker, every bottle — house-purchased or brought in — with its vintage, where it sits, and its full history.

Labels that last

Codes minted in durable batches: reprint an identical sheet any time, void a batch that never printed, reclaim a tag when a bottle leaves.

Storage spots

A locker is where a bottle belongs; the bar cooler is where it might sit tonight. Name your spots per location and every bottle knows both.

Find my bottle

The member shows a code; the server is walked to the exact bottle. A wrong bottle is refused before it ever leaves the cooler.

Roles and approvals

Servers submit pulls; managers approve. Each account carries exactly the permissions you give it — enforced by the database, not the screen.

Activity, append-only

A full chain of custody: every add, pull, request and change, with who and when. Filter by day, week, month, or your own range.

Catalog and tasting notes

Your list, per location, with prices and vintages. Horreum writes the tasting note for every wine — long-form, guest-ready, one voice.

Your brand, your locations

White-label from the first screen: your logo, your themes, your locker finishes. Add locations as you grow; each keeps its own catalog, staff and records.

For members

A collection worth showing off.

Members don’t get a spreadsheet. They get their cellar — with the story of every bottle in it.

  • The Collection. Every bottle with its tasting note, drink window and serving guidance, drawn as a cellar sheet they can print or share.
  • The Atlas. An interactive globe of the world’s wine regions, with the member’s own bottles pinned to where they were made.
  • History and ratings. What they have enjoyed, what they thought of it, and what came into the locker and how.
  • Show your server. One tap on a bottle shows the code that brings it to the table.
  • Requests and messages. Ask for a bottle from the list, or a word with the team, without picking up the phone.
  • An app, not a website. Installs on iPhone and Android in a minute; classic by day, midnight after dark.
